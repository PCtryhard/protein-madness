## 4. How we know it's any good — **the scoring stack, and the baseline we beat**

> _This is the section that differentiates us. Every team will have sequences;
> few can say anything defensible about them. Spend the time here._

The baseline is the **wild-type**, scored through exactly the same pipeline as
every design. Not a literature number, not a different tool — the same code
path, so the comparison is real.

<!-- auto:numbers_table -->
<!-- /auto -->

The four things to say, in this order:

1. **Catalytic integrity** — a design that lost a catalytic residue is dead
   regardless of every other number, so it is our one *hard* filter.
2. **Active-site pLDDT, not just mean pLDDT** — the global number hides exactly
   the thing we care about. A confident fold with a floppy active site is a
   failed design.
3. **PAE between the catalytic residues** — the same point, measured
   pairwise: are the catalytic residues confidently positioned *relative to each
   other*?
4. **Catalytic geometry** — distances and angles between the catalytic atoms
   versus the reference. This is the enzyme-specific metric the binder teams had
   no reason to build, and it is the one that makes this pitch credible. The
   ideal values came from the target config, which came from the challenge brief
   and the reference structure.

If asked "why should we believe your pLDDT?": we don't ask you to. pLDDT is one
of five filters, the hard one is chemical, and self-consistency RMSD is reported
alongside.
